What Whiplash Actually Does To Your Neck

In a rear-end collision, your body lurches forward while your head whips back, then snaps forward — all in a fraction of a second. That violent motion overstretches and tears the muscles, ligaments, and joint capsules of your neck, and can injure the discs and nerves too. The result is the classic whiplash picture: stiffness, pain, and limited motion.

Whiplash Symptoms To Watch For

  • Neck pain, stiffness, and reduced range of motion
  • Headaches, often starting at the base of the skull (cervicogenic headaches)
  • Shoulder, upper-back, or arm pain
  • Dizziness, fatigue, or trouble concentrating
  • Tingling or numbness in the arms

Important: these symptoms often don't appear until 24–72 hours after the crash. Feeling "fine" right after an accident does not mean you're uninjured.

Why Early Treatment Matters So Much

Whiplash that's treated promptly tends to resolve well. Whiplash that's ignored can settle into chronic neck pain, recurring headaches, and lasting stiffness — sometimes for years. Early, targeted care is the single biggest factor in a full recovery.

How We Treat Whiplash

  1. Thorough evaluation. We assess your neck's motion, the soft tissue, and rule out disc or nerve involvement — with imaging when indicated.
  2. Restore and relieve. Gentle adjustments and soft-tissue work restore motion and ease the strain, while laser therapy calms inflammation.
  3. Rebuild and document. Rehab restores strength and stability — and we document everything for your insurance and legal claim.
